Player's Handbook

The Pond Guide

Duck Homestead is a breeding sim first, but a pond is more than its genes. This is how the whole thing fits together — the daily loop, what a duck's life is made of, where the coins come from, how the competitions are won, who the rival ponds are, and what the long game looks like. The genetics guide covers the loci, standards, and breeding recipes in depth; this page points to it where it matters.

1 · Your first day

You start with 50 coins, four adult ducks (two drakes, two hens, each hiding a recessive surprise), 10 feed, 3 premium feed, 5 peas, and one medicine. Time runs at one game-minute per real second: a day is 24 real minutes at 1×, a season is six days, a year is 24. Night falls at 21:00 and the flock sleeps on the bank until 06:00; needs drift at 30% overnight, and a Sleep 'til dawn button skips to the morning.

Four needs, four tools

Hunger falls 6 points an hour (faster in winter and snow), cleanliness 3, happiness 2. Feed (+40) or premium (+60, +5 happy) from the Care menu — click the pond to scatter it. Rub a duck to pet it (+15 happiness, once an hour); pick the brush and rub to scrub it. Health drains only when a duck is starving (hunger under 20) or sick.

Sickness

A duck below 30 cleanliness risks 2% an hour; every sick pond-mate adds 1%. Untreated, a sick duck loses 4 health an hour — about a day to the end. Medicine (20 coins) cures on the spot. The Vet Clinic halves all of it.

The pond itself

Water fouls at 0.4 per duck per hour. Below 70% wild ducks stop visiting and a Scrub pond button appears; below 30% ducks dirty twice as fast. Rain freshens it; the Bog Filter halves fouling and the Waterfall cuts it by 30%.

Goals unlock the game

The Goals list on the left is a tutorial in disguise: pet the flock four times → Breeding; have an egg laid → Shop; hatch a duckling → Breed Book and Pond Derby. The Commissions Board is open from day one. Each goal pays coins; there are forty-odd of them, and the later ones stretch into year two.

Forage

Tap what appears on the grass: beetles (3 coins) and snails (2) by day, fireflies (1) at night, frogs (2) on the rim and dragonflies (3) over the water, moulted feathers (2, plus a Feather Album entry), duckweed (free feed), and the eggs your hens lay (into the basket). Feathers and duckweed never expire.

The dawn report

At 06:00 a card lists the day: a buyer at the gate, a wild visitor, cracking or cold eggs, courting pairs, the festival countdown, hungry ducks, the pond's state. Read it, then start the day.

2 · A duck's life

A duck's genes set its ceiling — see the genetics guide — but the life it leads decides where under that ceiling it lands. Here is the arc.

StageLastsWhat matters
EggHalf a day (Incubator: a quarter)Warmth starts at 70 and falls 10 an hour; tap the egg to tuck it in (+40, once an hour). Warmth sets incubation speed (0.5× cold → 1.3× toasty) and the hatchling's condition: a snug egg hatches at up to 90 hunger, 100 happiness, 100 health; a cold one at 40/45/70. Nesting boxes slow the loss 25% a level; elder hens and a broody hen slow it further. A cracked egg hatches itself after an hour if you don't tap it.
Duckling1 dayYellow fluff hides its true colours. It follows its mother and holds its cheer better within reach of an elder. What happens now is tallied for its marks (below). It can't race or train.
Juvenile2 daysColours revealed at the molt (and hardy or scrappy awarded). It can race and drill — racing or drilling now earns keen for life.
Adult11–17 days (14 × vigor)Breeds, lays, races, shows. Coming of age settles the rest of its marks. This is the productive life; the Brooder Lamp gets ducks here 20% sooner a level.
Elder4 daysPast breeding, but free: elders don't count against pond capacity. Elder hens sit broody by the nest (−25% egg-warmth loss each, two at most), and young ducks within 140 units of an elder lose happiness 30% slower. A life lived out here ends in an honoured passing: a feather for the album and 2–5 Society points by pedigree. Selling an elder forfeits that — the sell button says so.

Birthdays come every season (+10 happiness); a full year is a chronicle event. Each duck also has a secret favourite treat (peas, worms, or berries): the first time it eats it you get a heart burst, and from then on that treat restores 1.5× and adds 8 happiness.

Temperament

Two heritable loci (temper1–2) make a duck timid (0/4), steady (2/4), or bold (4/4). Bold ducks are busier, start races faster (about 10% over a timid duck), lay through a worse mood (happiness 45 needed instead of 65), win drake rivalries more often, and gain paddle 20% faster; timid ducks gain poise 20% faster. Every breed standard asks for one of the three. Sociability (social / loner) is the one quirk that stays personality, not genes.

Training

Every juvenile or older duck has three trained stats, 0–100, on its card:

StatDrillWhat it does at 100
PaddlePaddle drill — a rhythm game. A metronome ticks four counts, then eight beats roll toward a ring; tap as each arrives. Every tap is graded perfect, good, early, or late, and the form is their average.+15% race speed.
StaminaLong haul — a pacing game. Hold to paddle down a buoyed course; the puff gauge climbs while you hold and falls while you rest. Speed is full in the green, falls off above it, and a topped-out gauge stalls the duck for a second and a half. Form is lengths covered out of 4.5 in twelve seconds.+5% race speed, and boosts fade 35% slower down the track.
PoiseShow stance — a balance game. The duck wobbles on its show pedestal; nudge it left or right (arrow keys, A/D, or either half of the ring) to keep it in the mark, against a breeze, gusts, and a lean that grows over twelve seconds. Lean too far and it flaps. The judge glances four times, and those moments count triple.A parent's Egg Show care score is lifted by up to 50%, and the flock's poise counts in the Winter Lights parade.

A drill gains 2 (fumbled) to 16 (perfect) points — form is most of the gain — less as the stat nears 100, costs 8 hunger, cheers the duck 3, and pays 3 coins × form up to 15 coins a day. One drill per duck per day, +1 per Training Perch level. Every stat fades a point a day — a champion is kept, not just bred. Sick ducks, hungry ducks (under 30), and ducklings can't drill.

Life events

At 11:00 there is a 30% chance something needs a decision; a Decide chip lights in the HUD and a card opens. Close it to think; by 20:00 the flock settles it their own way.

A hen goes broody. Let her sit: every nest egg loses warmth half as fast today, she skips laying, and she's content (+8). Shoo her off: she lays as usual and sulks (−6). Unanswered, she sits.
Two drakes fall out. Let them settle it: the bolder one usually wins (+8 happiness, the proud mark); the loser takes −10 happiness and −6 health. Distract with treats (2 premium feed): both +6, nobody hurt. Send the challenger to the pen: needs a free pen place. Unanswered, they settle it.

3 · Running the flock

Breeding

Open Breed and click two adults on the pond (or use the chooser). Both must be adult, not sick, not penned, happiness over 50, health over 60, and rested — a nesting sets a 12-hour cooldown. The pair courts for one game-hour; at the end the hen lays with probability happiness × health (their averages, as fractions), +5% in spring, −6% per surplus drake. Feed and pet them during the courtship to tip it. Your very first clutch always takes. The nest holds 2 eggs (+2 per Nesting Box level); courting pairs count against it, so an egg pipeline keeps running even at a full pond.

Ducks that keep steady company become inseparable: bonded pairs seek each other out, cheer each other up close by, court 5% better, and a friend watching a drill is worth a point (and enjoys the show). Selling half of a bond breaks the other's heart — the card says so before you do. Close kin (parent–child, siblings) throw clutches whose vigor alleles have a 35% chance each of degrading. Every egg is stamped with two generations of ancestry, so the family tree survives the parents leaving.

Laying

Every adult or elder hen that's fed (hunger over 40) and content (see temperament) drops one unfertilised egg a day between 07:00 and 17:00, on the grass or the nearest bank. Tap it into the basket. Surplus drakes make her skip: each is a coin-flip against the egg. A broody hen skips her egg for the day.

Flock balance and the pen

Ducks keep best at one drake per three hens (two drakes are always fine). Each surplus drake drains hens' happiness 0.7 an hour and drakes' 0.3, costs clutches 6% viability, and halves laying — up to four surplus. The Flock panel shows the ratio. The Bachelor Pen (220, then 550 coins; 3 places a level) parks any adult out of the breeding population without selling: penned ducks don't lay, breed, race, or add pressure, and still need feeding and brushing.

Capacity

The pond holds 8 hatched ducks, +4 per Pond Expansion (100 / 250 / 500), +1 for the Society's Pondmaster perk, +1 per heritage pond. Eggs and elders don't count. Hatch over the limit and the pond is overcrowded: every extra duck adds 0.6 happiness loss an hour to everyone, the water fouls faster, and wild ducks stay away until you sell or expand.

Wild visitors

At 10:00, if the pond is over 70% clean, the flock averages 60+ happiness, and nothing is overcrowded, there's a 35% chance a wild duck lands (20% once you hold blue, pink bill, and crest). Each decoration you've placed lowers both bars by 3, up to five. The first visitor is guaranteed on day 2. Feed it three premium treats and it joins — bringing at least one rare gene; Society rank 5 makes the gift a true-breeding pair, rank 10 adds a chance of a near-standard duck for a breed your Book lacks, and a winter visitor at rank 10 can carry pink bill and crest together. It leaves the next day.

5 · Competition

The derby league

The Race button runs the daily derby: your duck against three wild racers, click on the meter's beat to paddle (precision matters — sloppy taps barely help). Speed = vigor × boldness × build (the sweet spot is slightly under medium size) × training × marks. Tiers: Pond Derby → County Derby → National Derby (show-standard ducks only, 60%+ match). Three net wins promote; three net losses relegate — second place is neutral only in the Pond Derby. Wins above Pond tier earn Society points equal to the tier.

The four festivals

Day 4 of each season. Every one can be won; a win raises next year's edition a tier — County, Regional, National — with +75% purse per tier and sharper opposition. A festival you don't finish packs up at 20:00.

Spring Egg Show. Enter one nest egg. Judges score rarity × 4 + standard match × 0.5 + care / 4, where care is the parents' average condition lifted by their poise. Against the three rival ponds' clutches and one local breeder. 1st/2nd earn 6/3 Society points × purse.
Summer Derby Grand Prix. A qualifying heat (fee 15; top two advance) and a final against the rival ponds' drilled racers — they get faster every year. Winning the final takes the tier.
Autumn Market Day. Three buyers, each smitten with one of your ducks, open 10–45% above (already ×1.5) list price. Haggle for +25% at a 55% risk of them walking; tap the pedestal to look the duck over first. Sell past the fair's target — 100 coins, +80 per tier — and the day is won (4 points × purse). Consumables are 20% off.
Winter Lights. Light five lanterns; the flock parades. Parade score = decorations (up to 6) × 8 + average poise × 0.3 + average happiness × 0.3, against a bar of 45 + 15 per tier. Then the fifth wish is yours: a stranger on the wind (the best wild visitor arrives tomorrow), good standing (+8 points), or a full purse (your biggest sale, 60–400). Happiness doesn't decay all day.

6 · The rival ponds

Three breeders keep ponds beside yours, each with a real flock of six genomes that breeds a generation on the first morning of every season and drills its birds. They start at 20% strength and gain 15% a year and 5% per win — so the world hardens with time, not just with your flock. Meet them in Shop → Society.

Marta of Millpond

Breeds to the standard and drills poise. Her eggs win shows.

Old Wiggins

A racing stable: bold, hardy lines and a lot of paddling.

The Reedy Sisters

Collectors of blue, pink bills, and crests — and dear stud fees.

Where you'll meet them: the Egg Show field, the Grand Prix grid, the Commissions Board (a third of postings once you have a reputation), the stud service on the Board (its three markets — commissions, eggs for sale, stud service — sit behind a row of pills, and every rival card folds its full gene readout behind Read the genes) — hire a rival's best drake for one clutch with a hen of yours; his genes are on show (the Scope reads them), the courtship runs as usual, and the egg's lineage remembers him — and the Society Cup — and the trade runs both ways: each rival sells one egg a day from a pairing of their own birds (Board → Eggs for sale), parents on show, shell a surprise, arriving as a gen-0 line.

The Commissions Board

Three slots, a new posting each morning at 08:00, six days to fill. Every three you fill raises the tier: sex demands from tier 1, minimum generation (1 → 5), standard match (60% → 80% → 90%), and from tier 3 a growing chance of a pink-bill clause. Pay scales to tier 6, points 3 + 3 × tier. If nobody fits, the card names the pair most likely to hatch one and the odds per egg.

The Society Cup

From rank 5, stake 40 points in any season to enter the year's Cup. From then on every point you earn counts against the rival ponds' seasonal tallies; on the last night of winter the highest takes it — 300 coins × the year for you, or a win on a rival's record. It is where points go once the ladder is climbed.

9 · The long game

  • The Breed Book — 60 breeds (colour × dilution × pattern × crest), each with a Pure / Standard / Master award: 180 awards. The Records tab tracks lifetime bests; the Chronicle writes the pond's history.
  • Festival tiers — carry each of the four to National; the rivals scale with you.
  • The Cup — the rival ponds get 15% stronger a year; a Cup win at year 3 is a different thing from one at year 1.
  • Heritage — once the Book has 10 breeds, retire the pond from the Save panel and refound it with one drake and one hen. Book, awards, Society, chronicle, records, rivals, and an open Cup carry over; the new pond starts with 50 + 100 × heritage coins and extra premium feed. Each retirement adds a pond slot and +1% mutation for the first five, then a quarter as much (to eight slots and +7.5%). Goals stay done and nothing re-locks.
  • Pedigree — generations bred on your pond (to 6) + fixed Book genes (to 4) + rare alleles carried (to 4) + purebred parents: 15 at most, 8% sale price a point. A gen-5 purebred with a family tree behind it is the thing this game is really about.

10 · Companion, keyboard, settings

Companion — Save → Companion & cloud sync → Link a device mints an 8-character code (10 minutes). Open /companion on a phone, enter it, and the pocket pond appears behind a touch UI: care and treats, quick drills (at a modest fixed form), the nest (pair, tuck, hatch, sell eggs), chores, the shop's upgrades, the Book and Chronicle, and the day's report with anything waiting on a decision — a broody hen, a wild visitor, a festival that needs the desktop. It installs to the home screen. The companion is a visitor: by default it only watches the cloud's latest copy while the desktop keeps playing. Take the reins (or tap any care button) to run the pond from the phone — the desktop pauses — and putting the phone down hands it straight back; the desktop carries on by itself within a few seconds.

Comparing ducks — Flock → Genes lays the whole flock out as a table, a column per trait, sortable by any header; tick a handful and choose Picked only to see just those side by side. Ctrl/Cmd-click any duck to pin its full card next to another.

Keyboard — Esc closes the open window (or cancels placing a decoration) · 1–6 Breed, Shop, Flock, Book, Race, Save · C duck cards · P pause · +/− speed · Space paddles in a race or drill · Ctrl/Cmd-click a duck pins its card to compare · ? settings. Every one of these can be rebound in Settings → Keyboard (Esc stays fixed); the list above shows the defaults.

Settings (the ★ button) — volume and mute (everything is synthesised; nothing downloads), reduced motion (follows the OS unless you say otherwise), large text, and the keyboard sheet. Settings live in the browser, not the save. The sparkle button on any duck's card takes a portrait photo to save, copy, or share.

Saving — the game autosaves every 30 seconds and only runs while the tab is visible (no offline progress). Export and import a save as text from the Save panel; a save that can't be read is stashed, never overwritten.
