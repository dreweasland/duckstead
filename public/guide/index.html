The Duck Genetics Guide

Every duck carries a diploid genome: thirteen loci, two alleles each, one from each parent. Nothing is hand-painted — plumage, build, bill, crest, and constitution are computed from these genes, so what you see on the pond is exactly what you can breed for.

0 · The five-minute version

Ignore the science words for a second. Every trait is just a pair of letter cards, and breeding is a tiny card game:

1 · Every duck holds two cards per trait

For crest, a duck might hold nR — one card came from its mother, one from its father. It holds a pair like this for all thirteen traits.

2 · The stronger card is the one you see

When the two cards differ, only the stronger one shows (the tables below rank them). The weaker card doesn't vanish — it hides, and the duck carries it invisibly its whole life.

3 · Breeding: each parent hands over ONE card, at random

Mum flips a coin and gives one of her two cards; dad does the same. The duckling's new pair is those two cards. That's the entire system — four equally likely combinations per trait.

4 · Surprises are hidden cards meeting

Two plain-looking parents each hiding an R have a 1-in-4 clutch chance both hand it over — and a crested duckling hatches from two crestless parents.

5 · Typos make the rare letters

Each handed-over card has a 2% chance of being miscopied into a different letter. That's the only way B blue and P pink bills appear out of nowhere.

6 · Some traits just count cards

Size, bill, and vigor have no strong or weak — you simply count the + cards across the pair(s). More + from the parents means more to hand down.

Worked through once: mum holds Dd, dad holds Dd. Each hands over one card at random, so the duckling is equally likely to be:

DD full colour Dd full colour, carries dilute dD full colour, carries dilute dd pastel!

So two full-colour carriers throw a pastel duckling once per four eggs, on average. Every recipe in this guide is this one trick applied to a different pair of letters.

1 · The thirteen loci

Mendelian loci — dominance decides what shows

LocusAlleles, dominant → recessiveWhat it does
Base colourM mallard = B blue > W white > k blackM and B are equal rank: an M/B duck is a blend — the Blue-Mallard. Blue is rare: absent from starters and shop stock.
DilutionD full > d dilutedd lightens plumage 40% — the pastel "Dilute" look.
PatternS solid > p spotted > c cappedSolid hides spotted; both hide capped. A capped duck is always cc.
MarkingsA dark > a paleColour of the spots or cap. Cosmetic — except that show standards call for one or the other.

Crestn plain > R crestRecessive: only RR grows the pom-pom. A single R is an invisible carrier.

The higher-ranked allele is expressed; equal ranks (only M vs B) blend. Recessives hide until two copies meet — which is why surprises hatch.

No dominance here — every + counts, so these respond to steady selection rather than lucky pairings.

2 · Inheritance

  • Each parent contributes one random allele per locus.
  • Mutation: each inherited allele has a 2% chance to flip — the only way B and P enter a closed flock, apart from wild visitors and Society stock. Heritage ponds add +1% per retirement (max +5%).
  • Starters carry no rare alleles but each hides at least one recessive (white/black, dilute, spotted/capped, or crest), so early clutches throw surprises.
  • Ducklings hide their colours under yellow fluff until the juvenile molt at one day old. The genes are set at lay time — the Scope reads them regardless.
  • Inbreeding: close kin (parent–child, siblings) give each + vigor allele a 35% chance of degrading to − in the clutch.

BBMB/MB/MMB/MB/M

3 · What the genes feed into

The Breed Book — 60 entries

A duck's breed is defined by four loci: base colour (five expressed colours, Blue-Mallard included) × dilution × pattern × crest. Discovery pays coins and Society points; each breed carries a Pure / Standard / Master award ladder.

Show standards — all thirteen loci

Every breed has a deterministic show standard: the Book loci homozygous (Blue-Mallard excepted — it wants the B/M pair), plus a per-breed build: size and bill-length targets, a bill colour (about a third want pink), a markings colour, and always 4/4 vigor. The card shows your % match over nine judged slots; ≥90% earns the Standard award. Egg Show judges weigh standard match as heavily as rarity; National derby races admit only ducks at 60%+.

Rarity (0–9) and pedigree (0–15)

Rarity: +2 each for expressed blue, pink bill, and crest; +1 each for size extreme, bill extreme, dilute. Pedigree: generations bred on your pond (≤6) + homozygous Book loci (≤4) + rare alleles carried (≤4) + 1 for purebred parents. Sale price scales with both — every pedigree point adds 8%.

Vigor in play

Race speed ×0.85–1.15, adult lifespan 11.2–16.8 days, up to −50% sickness chance, and a slot in every standard. The one stat with no downside — fix ++/++ into every line you keep. For racers, the build sweet spot is slightly under medium size — which conflicts with rarity's love of extremes. Temperament (energetic/mellow) is personality, not genetics: it can't be bred for.

4 · Reading genes

  • Free clues: swatches, pattern and crest chips, size/bill/vigor gauges on every card.
  • Pedigree Scope (shop, 150) shows exact allele pairs — expressed bright M, masked dim k — plus "carries" chips for hidden recessives.
  • Breeding Advisor: key breeder (sole carrier of a rare gene), worth keeping (reaches Book entries nobody else can), best of breed (closest to its standard), or covered — safe to sell.
  • Breed panel: exact per-trait odds for any pairing, close-kin warnings, and how many undiscovered breeds a pairing could unlock.

5 · Getting the rare genes

  • Wild visitors always bring blue, pink bill, or a true crest. Society rank upgrades the gifts — true-breeding pairs at rank 5, near-standard ducks for missing breeds at rank 10, and a winter visitor can carry pink bill and crest together. The Winter Lights "stranger on the wind" wish summons the best available.
  • Mutation — 2% per allele means roughly one duckling in twelve carries some novelty; heritage pushes it higher.
  • Commissioned stock — Society rank 8 lets you order a duck with a chosen rare gene outright.

6 · Breeding recipes

Fix a gene. Pair two carriers, keep homozygous offspring. Without the Scope: a crested duck is always RR, a capped one always cc, and a blue from two non-blue parents carries exactly one B.
Chase a standard. Cross your best % match with a mate strong where it's weak — the standard card's per-slot chips show exactly which loci to fix. Partial matches are valuable parents even if they'll never show.
Keep a bloodline clean. Rotate two pairs, or park a spare drake in the Bachelor Pen — his genes stay available without adding drake pressure.
Mind the ratio. One drake per three hens (two always tolerated). Surplus drakes harry the hens: happiness, laying, and clutch viability all suffer — 6% viability per extra drake.

Viability isn't genetics — it's care: happiness × health of the pair at lay time (spring +5%). Egg warmth likewise shapes hatch speed and hatchling condition, never the genes.
